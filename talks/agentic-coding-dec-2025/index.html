Your Expertise Matters More Than Ever

We actually need you to be a great engineer, researcher, or PM more than ever…

  • Your job is changing, but you can put yourself in charge of how it changes
  • Following instructions, no matter how technical, is no longer that valuable
  • Your cognition, curiosity, and expertise is what makes the difference between vibe coding and building real software
  • Agentic Coding requires you to lean into how to teach your craft

"But I tried this stuff and it screwed up!"

Most likely you are holding it wrong. I mean it.

  • Off-the-shelf Copilot, Claude, Codex is unlikely to give you good results
  • We are in the era of craftspeople building their own tools
  • Your expertise took years to build. Now you need to invest in how to codify it
  • Think about the AI as a very smart, somewhat forgetful intern

→ On Writing by Stephen King

Thinking About Thinking

  • How do smart groups of human experts organize complex work?
  • What problem am I trying to solve?
  • What outcome do I want?
  • What is the thought process that gets me to a quality solution?
  • Can I make that process into a tool?
  • How to structure an agentic pattern to aim at the desired outcome?
  • How do you build reliability into an untrustworthy system?

What is Agentic Engineering?

  • It's what you make of it
  • It's an opportunity
  • It's an exploration
  • It's taking all the same things we did before, as a team, and trying to scale them to guidelines, processes, and guardrails for AI agents
  • How can you use agents as design elements in your process?

"But I don't trust it"

You should NOT trust it. Use that. Lean into it.

  • What system would you build to find all the places it failed, lied, or made a mistake?
  • Pit multiple AI against one another with the explicit mission of not trusting one another
  • Skepticism is an asset. Embrace it.
  • Recursion, depth, and systems of checks and balances keep us productive and safe

"But I built my expertise over years… How will new people get that skill?"

  • My first programming language was assembler on an 8bit CPU. I can't do that anymore — but it helped me understand each thing that came after
  • New practitioners must exercise critical thinking, tinkering, reasoning — more than ever before
  • The ones that succeed will take things apart to learn how they work
  • Eval is an excellent place to build skills — it forces structured thinking

Crawl, Walk, Run is Going Away

You must lead with high ambition — Explore, Reach, Refine

  • The cost of failure is trending toward zero
  • The upside of ambitious experiments is high
  • We all need to lean in to reaching for things that might not be (yet) possible
  • The most impactful advances have come from reaching for big changes

What is the loop that gets you to green?

  • Should you spend more time on articulating your processes than on code review itself?
  • Convergence on quality is driven by:
  • Layers of recursion
  • Structured workflows
  • Continually refined quality gates
  • Eval across distributions

Goal Seeking Agents as Nodes in a Workflow

AI Advantage: Think at scale in parallel

  • What aspects of your workflow can be explored across a distribution of possible paths?
  • Fan out, aggregate, summarize…
  • Goal-seeking agents as tools, but also part of the system
  • How many can you build to help you?
